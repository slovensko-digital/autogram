# Autogram: electronic-signature legal obligations and compliance evidence

> **Branch scope:** this is a documentation-only branch. The trusted-list validation hardening and the three tests named in the text (SignatureValidatorTrustedListTest, TrustedListLiveSmokeTest, SignatureValidationPresentationTest) are delivered as a separate code change and are not present on this branch; those mentions describe that companion change.

**Assessment date:** 3 October 2026. **Scope:** the Autogram desktop application in this repository, including its locally hosted API and CLI; not the browser extension, a hosted signing/validation platform, or a separate trust service. **Issue:** [#715](https://github.com/slovensko-digital/autogram/issues/715). This is a source-code and publicly available documentation assessment, **not** a legal opinion or an audit of a production release.

**Issue-discussion update:** The [author's comment of 3 October 2026](https://github.com/slovensko-digital/autogram/issues/715#issuecomment-5966439821) points to the Slovak National Security Authority's [catalogue of trust-service legislation](https://www.nbu.gov.sk/1619-sk/doveryhodne-sluzby/). This report cross-checks the catalogue **by addressee**, not by assuming every listed act imposes duties on Autogram; the additional acts and explicit exclusions appear in §4a. As of the assessment, the issue contains no other substantive comment or implementation decision.

## How to read the findings

The project representative confirmed that the organisation is **not a qualified trust-service provider**, does not operate a remote service receiving users' documents/personal data as part of Autogram, **publishes releases under its own name**, and charges **above cost recovery** for support while describing releases as non-commercial. Those facts are used below, but the legal entity's identity, contracts, actual releases and business arrangements were not independently verified. In particular, an open-source licence or a free download does not by itself settle whether the publisher makes the software available as part of commercial activity.

**Status:** **Met** = the cited repository evidence demonstrates the stated technical measure (not independent standards certification); **Not met** = a demonstrable gap against an applicable requirement; **Not applicable** = the provision addresses another actor/activity under the confirmed scope; **Unknown** = applicability or actual compliance cannot be established from the evidence. An entry marked **Not applicable** can still carry an interoperability recommendation. An entry marked **Unknown** is **not** an allegation of non-compliance. Entries distinguish a *legal duty on the organisation* from a *technical criterion for a signature/validation result*. Legal dates are **not** necessarily software release deadlines.

## 2. Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1945 — validation reference standards

**Law:** [2025/1945, Art. 1 and Annexes I–II](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/1945/oj/eng), adopted under eIDAS Arts. 32(3), 32a(3), 40 and 40a; **entered into force 20 October 2025** (Art. 2). Annex I covers qualified signatures/seals and Annex II advanced signatures/seals based on qualified certificates. The Annexes identify and adapt ETSI validation specifications (including TS 119 172-4 and TS 119 102-2); check their *cited versions and adaptations*, not just an ETSI title.

**Important application-specific wording:** *Both annexes* replace clause 4.3, REQ-4.3-02, with **“Signature validation applications shall be compliant with ETSI TS 119 101 [13]”** (cited version **V1.1.1 (2016-03)**). This is an explicit requirement **within the referenced validation-standard route** for applications—not merely a rule for QTSPs. Art. 1 establishes the reference specifications used for the **presumption of compliance** under eIDAS Arts. 32(1) and 32a(3); do not misstate the annex as a separate requirement that all desktop apps obtain certification. Read [the authoritative act and annexes](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/1945/oj/eng) together with [eIDAS Arts. 32(1)–(3), 32a(1)–(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/910/2024-10-18/eng).

## Priorities and evidence needed to close the unknowns

1. **Validation correctness (E3–E5, V1–V4, N2):** treat eIDAS Arts. 32(2) and 32a(2) as **direct validation-system result/security-issue rules**, not merely QTSP obligations. Test qualified/advanced signatures **and seals** from every EU trusted list, expired/revoked/unknown certificates, QSCD evidence, pseudonyms, historical signing times, offline/partial-list states, missing revocation data, timestamps and ambiguous `INDETERMINATE` results. Map **the entire application**, not just DSS 6.5, to the **2025/1945 Annexes I–II** and their ETSI TS 119 101 application requirement if claiming the reference-standard presumption; test 24-hour signing-certificate revocation freshness and failed-applicability reporting. **SK TLv6 now passed a live smoke test; only selected countries are checked** and neither that result nor the fixed all-selected-lists health check proves full EU validation. See the [gap/test matrix](eidas-2-gap-matrix.md).
2. **Public-sector format compatibility (F1–F2):** produce a matrix of output/input profiles and version numbers against **2026/248 Annexes I–II**; test CAdES/XAdES/PAdES/ASiC, assess whether JAdES needs creation/validation support for actual users, and preserve pre-cutoff legacy validation. This is a product-interoperability decision, **not** an automatic legal duty to implement JAdES by 2027.
3. **CRA classification (C1–C5):** obtain legal-entity/release-brand details, commercial distribution and support contracts, evidence of systematic stewardship, product classification under Annex III/[2025/2392](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/2392/oj/eng), documented support period, vulnerability-disclosure and incident-reporting policies. Only then determine which conditional controls apply and whether the **already-effective** manufacturer reporting duty is triggered.
4. **Accessibility, data-flow and national review (E11, G1–G2, S1–S3):** determine whether Autogram is an end-user product used in the provision of an eIDAS service, then assess plain-language/accessibility duties; check actual update/support/download/TSA/OCSP traffic, log retention and privacy notices; have Slovak counsel verify the **current consolidated** §§ 23–28, Decree 78/2020 and specific receiving-authority formats. Review a production binary/release, not only source code.

**Conclusion:** Under the confirmed local-software/non-QTSP scope, most *new provider and wallet obligations do not directly attach to Autogram*. **That does not exempt its validation system:** eIDAS Arts. 32(2), 32a(2) and the seal counterparts address the correctness and security-relevant transparency of the results it gives relying parties; 2025/1945 explicitly names **signature validation applications** in its reference-standard annexes. **One live SK TLv6 test passed and selected-list health/presentation safeguards were added**, but qualified-signature outcomes across countries and edge cases, conditional eIDAS Art. 15 accessibility, and **2026/248 public-sector interoperability** remain unverified. There is not enough evidence to honestly mark overall eIDAS or CRA compliance **Met** or **Not met**. No specific applicable statutory violation was demonstrated by this repository-only review; see the [gap/test matrix](eidas-2-gap-matrix.md).
