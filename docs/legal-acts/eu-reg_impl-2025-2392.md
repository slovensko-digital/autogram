# Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392 — technical descriptions of important and critical products with digital elements (CRA)

## Executive summary

This act gives the **technical descriptions of the categories of important products with digital elements** (Annex I, classes I and II) and **critical products with digital elements** (Annex II) listed in Annexes III and IV to Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Act) and adopted under Article 7(4) of that Regulation. It does not itself impose obligations: it defines when a product's **core functionality** meets a category, which then routes the manufacturer to the stricter or mandatory conformity-assessment procedures of CRA Articles 32 and following. Its relevance to Autogram is therefore **Conditional / Unknown**, not a direct software duty.

Autogram is a desktop/CLI/API tool whose core functionality, on inspected evidence, is **creating and validating electronic signatures and seals over documents**, not any of the listed network, endpoint, identity-management or hardware categories ([README.md](../../README.md), line 4; [DssSigningParametersFactory.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/core/DssSigningParametersFactory.java), lines 14–27). Applying recital 5's ability-versus-core-functionality test, the closest technical proximities are all **input, integration or supply-chain facts, not matches**: it consumes eID/qualified identity credentials and qualified-signature cards ([DefaultDriverDetector.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/core/DefaultDriverDetector.java), lines 36–75), it validates certificates and trusted lists ([SignatureValidator.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/core/SignatureValidator.java), lines 55–56, 76–81), and it embeds a JavaFX browser engine for rendering ([SigningDialogController.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/ui/gui/SigningDialogController.java), line 66; [pom.xml](../../pom.xml), lines 401–405). Recital 3 expressly says integrating an embedded browser does **not** make the host application a browser. A cryptographic signing application must **not** be assumed to be an important or critical product.

Because the organisation releases Autogram **under its own name and charges above cost recovery for support**, CRA commercial-supply / manufacturer classification remains **Unknown with elevated manufacturer risk** — but that unresolved role does not by itself put Autogram into any category described here. The technical-description question and the manufacturer-role question are independent; both remain unresolved, and no statutory breach is inferred from missing evidence.

The CRA obligations the descriptions feed mostly apply from **11 December 2027** (Article 14 from 11 September 2026). ## Legend and provision links

**Relevance:** Direct · Conditional · Indirect · None. **Status:** Done · Not done · Unknown · Ignored (context, or out of the confirmed scope; never "knowingly disregarded"). Relevance and status are independent.

Every provision row links to the official text at that unit — article anchors `#art_1`, `#art_2`, `#art_3`, annex anchors `#anx_I`, `#anx_II` and recital anchors `#rct_1`–`#rct_9` on the [official OJ page](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2025/2392/oj). Annex I and Annex II carry no per-category sub-anchors in the source, so each category row links to its annex anchor; the category/entry label itself (e.g. `Class I, point 12(a)`) identifies the unit. Preamble units that have no publisher anchor link to the act page.

**Reading rule applied below.** Under this act a category is met only when the product's **core functionality** matches the technical description (recitals 2, 4, 5). Categories whose descriptions do not touch signing/verification at all are **None / Ignored**. Categories Autogram touches as an input, integration or supplier are **Indirect / Unknown**. Only where a genuine classification question remains ("PKI and digital certificate issuance software") is **Conditional / Unknown** used. `Unknown` for those rows records that both the technical-description match and the underlying CRA manufacturer classification are unresolved.

## Coverage and limitations

- **Text obtained:** the complete official **English** act, including both annexes, from the Publications Office XHTML manifestation of CELEX `32025R2392` by content negotiation (HTTP 200, 78,082 bytes), converted to text locally. Its anchor identifiers (`rct_1`–`rct_9`, `art_1`–`art_3`, `anx_I`, `anx_II`) were read from the manifest and are the scheme used in every provision link. No authentic OJ PDF was downloaded or independently verified; the XHTML is the original published version, and a full subsequent amendment/corrigendum history check remains outstanding.
- **Enumerated independently from the official act:** 9 recitals; preamble opening + 2 having-regard paragraphs; Article 1's 2 definitions; Article 2's 2 numbered paragraphs; Article 3's 2 unnumbered paragraphs; 1 adoption/signature formula. These produce **54 assessment rows**: 12 preamble/recital rows, 7 article/concluding rows, 24 Annex I Class I rows, 7 Annex I Class II rows and 4 Annex II rows.
- **Annex enumeration:** Annex I Class I has **19 numbered categories** and Class II has **4**; Annex II has **3**. Where one numbered category states multiple distinct product types in separate descriptions, the row is split to keep each description individually identifiable while retaining the category number — Class I points 12 (routers/modems/switches), 15 (ASIC/FPGA), 18 (toys: social/location) and 19 (wearables: health/children); Class II points 1 (hypervisors/container runtimes) and 2 (firewalls/IDS/IPS); Annex II point 3 (secure elements/smartcards). Recital 3's embedded-browser rule and recital 5's ability-versus-core-functionality rule are the interpretive basis for the `Indirect`/`Conditional` treatment of the browser, identity-management and PKI rows.
- **Relevance/status distribution:** no row is `Direct` or `Done`/`Not done`; this act creates category descriptions, not duties a software build can complete. Proximity rows are `Indirect`/`Unknown` (identity management, browsers, Hardware Devices with Security Boxes, secure elements, smartcards) or `Conditional`/`Unknown` (PKI and digital certificate issuance software, plus recitals 2 and 5 and Articles 2(1)–(2)); all remaining categories are `None`/`Ignored` with the core-functionality reason stated.
- **Not inspected:** the full texts of the cited in-turn instruments were not re-read here — CRA Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (assessed separately in [eu-reg-2024-2847.md](eu-reg-2024-2847.md)), Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482 (EUCC) and its Common Criteria / Common Evaluation Methodology references, Regulation (EU) 2019/881, Directive 2009/48/EC, Regulations (EU) 2017/745/2017/746 and Directive (EU) 2019/944. The AVA_VAN level definitions in particular are external standards and were not inspected. Recital 8's cross-reference to 2024/482 is not reproduced or imported as text of this act.
- **Outstanding evidence:** a documented CRA role/manufacturer classification decision; a core-functionality analysis of Autogram as actually supplied (desktop, HTTP API and CLI, including optional token/keystore providers); the provider/token/hardware inventory for the supply-chain rows; and the CRA essential-requirements risk assessment described in recital 6.
