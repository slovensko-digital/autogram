# Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1571 — supervisory-body annual reports

## Executive summary

This act specifies annual reporting by the designated **European Digital Identity Wallet and trust-service supervisory bodies**, not by an independent signing-application publisher. Under the [register's shared facts](README.md#shared-facts), Autogram's publisher is not a qualified trust-service provider and operates no Autogram service receiving users' documents or personal data; no supervisory-body designation is established. Provider status would not itself make the publisher the reporting authority. Consequently, the reporting requirements below are **None / Ignored** for this application scope, not unfinished application features.

Reports about trusted lists are indirectly relevant to the ecosystem Autogram consumes, but a successful SK TLv6/EU LOTL test on 3 October 2026 does not establish any authority's reporting compliance or EU-wide validation correctness. No authority's designation instrument, annual report, submission receipt or underlying supervisory records was reviewed. Adopted 29 July 2025, published 30 July 2025, this act entered into force on **19 August 2025**; it sets no separate application date or vendor implementation deadline.

## Coverage and limitations

- Obtained the **complete original official English XHTML** from the Publications Office CELEX resource: title, preamble, six recitals, two articles, final declaration/signature material, Annexes I and II, and all six bibliographic footnotes (five in the main text, one in Annex II). Full legal text is linked, not reproduced here.
- Checked enumeration against the retrieved XHTML: **recitals (1)–(6); Article 1(1)–(4); Article 2's one unnumbered paragraph; final binding/direct-applicability declaration; Annex I introductory paragraph and points (1)–(12), including (5)(a)–(e); Annex II introductory paragraph and points (1)–(14), including (5)(a)–(f)**. Each is assessed above; annex introduction assessments precede their tables. Parent inspection-summary requirements and their lettered fields are both identified. Name/address/contact and organisation/structure/resources/capabilities have separate descriptive field rows. Other compound, unlettered descriptions retain every stated component within their point's row; the act provides no additional numbered template cells or definitions.
- Preamble citations are assessed as legal context. Adoption location/date and Commission signature identify promulgation, not additional duties. Footnotes identify referenced acts and were checked; they are not additional reporting fields. No recital, operative paragraph, lettered annex field or annex point of the obtained act was omitted.
- **Version limitation:** coverage is complete for the obtained original act, not a verified 8 October 2026 consolidation or exhaustive amendment/corrigendum history. EUR-Lex's empty responses prevented an independent current-version check through those endpoints.
- No ETSI standard is expressly referenced in this act; **no ETSI standards were inspected** for this document. Referenced eIDAS, NIS2, data-protection legislation and Decision 2015/1505 are separate works; their complete provisions were not imported into this assessment.
- No source-code audit or build was performed: the decisive issue is the authority addressee, not application implementation. Shared non-QTSP/no-hosted-document-service facts do not prove exclusion from every eIDAS provision, non-qualified trust-service role or Article 15 end-user-product scenario. Those questions are outside this reporting act.
- Outstanding evidence for any authority-facing assessment: designation/competence instruments, Commission channel and format documentation, reports/submission receipts, supervisory and inspection files, incident notifications, cooperation records, termination-plan checks, trusted-list availability/update records, accreditation schemes and any State-requested trust infrastructure. Missing these records is not proof of a statutory violation. Reassess if the organisation is actually designated as a supervisory body or contracted to operate that body's reporting workflow; a provider role alone is insufficient.
