# Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 — Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)

## Executive summary

Autogram is software with network connections, a local signing API and security-sensitive document processing. CRA applicability is **Conditional / Unknown**, not excluded merely because the source is open. The organisation confirms own-name releases and support charged above cost recovery: these facts elevate commercial-supply/manufacturer risk, but contracts, the relationship between support and supply, and the legal classification have not been established. A documented role decision is urgent: **Article 14 applies from 11 September 2026**, including in-scope products placed on the market before general application (Article 69(3)). Most other requirements apply from **11 December 2027**; Chapter IV applies from **11 June 2026**.

Inspected source proves XML hardening, an available-version query, configurable HTTP/HTTPS endpoints and permissive CORS. It does **not** prove a complete risk assessment, secure update distribution, release SBOM, support commitment, reporting capability, conformity assessment or organisational compliance. No statutory breach is inferred from missing evidence while the manufacturer classification remains Unknown.

Article 24 is a separate, conditional steward regime, not a blanket exemption or an alternative chosen by any open-source manufacturer. Article 3(14) expressly excludes manufacturers from the steward definition. Article 24(1) requires a verifiable security policy, including vulnerability handling and encouragement of voluntary reporting; (2) requires authority cooperation and provision of that policy on reasoned request; (3) applies Article 14(1) where the steward participates in development, and Article 14(3) and (8) where severe incidents affect steward-provided development systems. Article 71 expressly accelerates Article 14, **not Article 24**: do not infer that all Article 24 duties commenced in September 2026. Obtain a legal decision on the cross-reference's temporal operation if steward status is claimed. Article 64(10)(b) excludes steward infringements from the specified administrative fines, not from duties or corrective action under Article 52(3).

## Coverage and limitations

The original official English XHTML was obtained with its complete preamble, **130 recitals, Articles 1–71, and Annexes I–VIII**. Tables identify individual numbered paragraphs, definitions, lettered/roman subpoints, subsequent unnumbered operative blocks, amendment insertions, transitional/application provisions, annex list items, module subpoints and declaration signature fields. Parent introductions/headings are structural rather than duplicate duties. Citation footnotes, signing-off names and the separate OJ C statement link are not treated as independent obligations.

Coverage is checked against the official XHTML's `rct_1`–`rct_130`, `art_1`–`art_71`, numbered-paragraph containers, nested list tables and annex parts, not against the older compliance report. This covers the **original published text only**. It is not a current consolidated-law/version-history completeness assertion. Outstanding authoritative sources include later amendment/corrigendum inventory, current important-category technical descriptions, notification-format/dissemination measures, applicable OJ-listed standards and national enforcement/designation records.

Referenced ETSI, harmonised standards, common specifications, EU certification schemes and the separate OJ C statement were **not inspected as complete works**. No ETSI conformity is claimed. Repository inspection is limited to the precise XML/API/update paths above and does not prove all call sites, release binaries, dependency security, deployment defaults, regression-test outcomes or institutional processes. No hosted document service or QTSP role is inferred, but those facts do not decide CRA commercial supply. Manufacturer/steward classification, reportable events and their awareness dates, release SBOMs/security tests, funded support periods, secure update mechanisms, conformity files, contacts and authority-response procedures remain outstanding.
