# Directive (EU) 2016/2102 — public-sector web and mobile accessibility

## Executive summary

**Indirect relevance to Autogram's desktop application, local API and CLI; no blanket accessibility obligation on its independent publisher follows from this Directive.** The Directive addresses Member States and the websites and mobile applications of public sector bodies. A native desktop signing application is not, merely because a public authority uses or recommends it, that authority's website or a mobile application within Article 3(2). The local HTTP API does not itself establish a public-body website deployment.

Public-sector online signing integrations nevertheless need careful assessment: recital 19 includes forms, authentication and identification processes, and outsourcing does not automatically establish the third-party-content exclusion. A public body may need accessible integrated journeys and alternatives; procurement specifications, contracts and national legislation may impose requirements on a supplier independently of this Directive. Those facts and end-to-end accessibility evidence are **Unknown**. This document does not certify accessibility or infer a violation from missing tests.

Adopted **26 October 2016**, published **2 December 2016**, entered into force **22 December 2016**. Transposition was due **23 September 2018**; application was staged on **23 September 2019**, **23 September 2020** and **23 June 2021**. These are Member-State/public-body dates, not autonomous desktop-vendor deadlines. Assessment: **8 October 2026**; repository baseline **5ca91d5c**. Scope and table terminology follow the [register README](README.md). This is a reading aid, not legal advice.

### Evidence and missing evidence used in the tables

- **E1 — native desktop implementation:** [`GUIApp.start`, lines 20–50 and 76–80](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/ui/gui/GUIApp.java) creates a JavaFX application/stage and scene, with an optional local server. This establishes architecture, not screen-reader, keyboard or visual accessibility.
- **E2 — HTTP integration surface:** [`AutogramServer.start`, lines 35–71; `buildServer`, lines 74–81](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/server/AutogramServer.java) registers information, certificate, documentation, signing, batch and asset routes and binds to a configured host. HTTP functionality does not prove public-body operation or an accessible online journey.
- **D1 — deployment and actor evidence needed:** public-body identity/status under Article 3(1), actual website/mobile deployment, public availability, who develops/funds/controls each component, integration diagram and responsibility agreement. No in-scope public-body website/mobile app operated by this publisher is established by the register facts.
- **D2 — accessibility evidence needed:** applicable national rules and current OJ standard citation; contractual/procurement criteria; page/app/process inventory; keyboard, assistive-technology, visual and usability tests of the actual signing journey (including handoff, errors and return); accessible document/output and alternative-process evidence. No such audit was performed here.
- **D3 — public-body governance evidence needed:** published accessibility statement and revision history, feedback/response records, assessed exceptions and alternatives, disproportionate-burden decision and enforcement contact. These are not duties to invent for the desktop publisher.
- **D4 — institutional evidence needed:** national transposition/designation notices, official monitoring reports, Commission implementing/delegated acts, review and current consolidated EU metadata. Institutional performance is outside a repository audit.

## Coverage and limitations

- **Obtained and checked:** the complete English **original EU Official Journal text, OJ L 327/1–15**, obtained as the preserved OJ PDF from legislation.gov.uk and read through its final Article 15/signatures. Its content and numbering were cross-checked against the National Archives' original-as-adopted HTML, including the end of the fetched HTML after the initial display truncation. Direct EUR-Lex retrieval yielded no readable content; this limits current-version verification, not access to the original published act.
- **Enumerated:** all **56 recitals**, **15 articles**, **51 numbered article paragraphs/definition points** (including Article 3's eight definitions), every **27 lettered subpoint**, both **Article 1(4)(f)(i)–(ii)** alternatives, and all **12 unnumbered subparagraphs** inside Articles 6(2), 6(3), 7(1), 8(5) and 12(1). Each unnumbered single-paragraph Article 2, 4, 13, 14 and 15 is separate. List-only chapeaux are structural headings, including Article 1(4)(f); Article 3's introductory definition chapeau and Article 7(1)'s fourth subparagraph are identified explicitly. Preamble legal-basis/procedure paragraphs and closing authentication are also identified. There are **zero annexes**. Footnotes were read as bibliographic/procedural references, not treated as standalone operative duties or reproduced in full.
- **No omitted provision of the obtained original:** tables paraphrase rather than reproduce legal text. Full coverage is claimed only for this obtained original, **not an unverified 2026 consolidation**. Current EU amendment/corrigendum/repeal status and current OJ standard citations remain outstanding. The National Archives explicitly does not apply post-2020 EU amendments to these Directive copies; its archive alone cannot settle those questions.
- **Standards and related instruments not inspected:** no EN 301 549 edition, ETSI text, WCAG complete specification, standard-citation decision, model-statement decision, monitoring/reporting decision, procurement Directive, UN convention or national transposition statute was reviewed in full for this document. Their requirements are not silently incorporated or claimed satisfied. The 2015 standard reference is the original act's fallback, not a claim that it is the currently cited harmonised version.
- **Outstanding product/organisational evidence:** D1–D3 remain necessary for any actual public-sector integration or possible national/contractual duty. No released binary, keyboard/screen-reader journey, accessible output, statement, reasonable-accommodation decision or public-body burden assessment was audited. E1/E2 establish only the inspected desktop/API architecture. The source lines cited are inspected working-tree evidence; baseline 5ca91d5c is the register reference, not a claim that every inspected working-tree line was compared with that commit.
- **Result boundary:** accessibility of an integrated public-sector signing service matters, but the service owner's duties must not be converted into an unsupported direct vendor obligation. Conversely, the Directive's limited product scope must not be used to dismiss procurement, contractual, national-law or reasonable-accommodation requirements that need separate evidence.
