# Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 (eIDAS), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 — "eIDAS 2.0"

**Register:** [Autogram legal-act register](README.md) · **Assessment date:** 8 October 2026 · **Repository baseline:** `5ca91d5c` · **Scope:** Autogram desktop application, locally hosted API and CLI, not the browser extension, a hosted platform or a trust service operated by the publisher.

## Executive summary

**Why this act matters most.** eIDAS is the founding act of the whole framework; every other act in this register amends, implements or depends on it. Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 ("eIDAS 2.0") did not replace eIDAS: it rewrote many articles and added the European Digital Identity Wallet rules. The current binding text is therefore the **consolidated** Regulation 910/2014 (in force as at 18 October 2024, with a corrigendum of 9 April 2025).

**What actually reaches Autogram.** The organisation is not a qualified trust-service provider and runs no service receiving users' documents. Most obligations here address Member States, their supervisory bodies, wallet providers and qualified trust-service providers, and are **None / Ignored** for Autogram. Two groups do reach an independent signing/validation application:

1. **Validation-system duties that do not depend on being a trust-service provider.** Articles **32(1)–(2)**, **32a(1)–(2)** and, through Article 40/40a, the seal counterparts require a system that validates a QES (and an advanced signature/seal based on a qualified certificate) to check the certificate, issuer, validity at signing time, validation data, signatory data, pseudonym indication, the QSCD point (only for QES), integrity, AdES conditions, and to give the relying party the **correct result** and let it detect security-relevant issues. Autogram performs these checks through DSS (`SignatureValidator`, GUI badges), but **correct outcomes across countries, times and edge cases are Unknown**: no licensed qualified fixtures, no cross-country coverage beyond the selected-list product decision, and no proof of the 24-hour revocation and indeterminate-reporting behaviour.
2. **Conditional accessibility, Art. 15.** If, and only if, a release is an "end-user product used in the provision of" eID identification or a trust service, plain-language and accessibility duties attach. Whether Autogram is deployed that way is **Unknown**; the developer not being a QTSP does **not** by itself exclude Art. 15.

**What we already demonstrated.** A live test on 3 October 2026 validated the EU LOTL and the current Slovak TLv6, and trusted-list health is now derived from the LOTL plus every selected list's status, with conservative badges when a list is missing (`SignatureValidatorTrustedListTest`, `SignatureValidationPresentationTest`). That proves one list and one presentation rule, **not** EU-wide or historical QES validation correctness.

**Dates that matter.** eIDAS 2.0 in force 20 May 2024 (amendment) / consolidated reading 18 October 2024; corrigendum 9 April 2025; remote-QSCD management qualified status and old-certificate transitional rules run to **21 May 2026 / 21 May 2027**; several Commission implementing-act deadlines fall on 21 May 2025 and 21 November 2024 and concern the Commission, not Autogram. None is automatically an Autogram release deadline.

**Bottom line.** Overall compliance cannot be marked **Met**. For the validation-system duties the honest status is **Unknown** with concrete, testable gaps; for the wallet, provider and Member-State chapters it is largely **Not applicable / Ignored** under the confirmed scope. Source-code review cannot prove or disprove conformity; independent qualified fixtures and a production-release audit are required.

**Text obtained.** The consolidated enacting terms and Annexes I–VII were taken from the Publications Office/Cellar consolidation `02014R0910 — EN — 18.10.2024 — 002.003` on 8 October 2026; the 77 base recitals and 78 amending recitals were taken from the respective OJ acts (`32014R0910?language=eng`, `32024R1183?language=eng`). The consolidated Cellar XHTML explicitly omits the preamble; recitals therefore come from the two OJ acts, which is the authoritative source.

**How enumeration was checked.** Article headings were extracted as standalone lines (82 headings, from `Article 1` to `Article 52`, including the lettered insertions). Recital numbering was parsed from `Whereas:` to the enacting formula (77 and 78 respectively). A unit may appear in more than one cross-reference in the source; the heading list, not occurrence order, drives the table. Articles 17–19 are **not present** as headings in this consolidation; this is recorded, not silently omitted, and no text was invented for them.

**What is not evidenced here.** (1) Correct validation **outcomes** for real qualified signatures/seals across countries and signing times; only the trusted-list *sources* and selected-list health are tested. (2) The 24-hour signing-certificate revocation freshness and failed-applicability/indeterminate reporting required by the 2025/1945 route (see the [2025/1945 document](eu-reg_impl-2025-1945.md)). (3) Whether any Autogram release is an "end-user product used in the provision of" an eID/trust service, which decides whether Art. 15 applies. (4) Whether the referenced ETSI standards were ever applied to the application as a whole; those standards are separate works, not part of this act's text.

**Effect on statuses.** Where an article addresses Member States, supervisory bodies, QTSPs, wallet providers, browsers or attribute/ledger/archiving providers, it is **None / Ignored** under the confirmed scope. Where it addresses the *validation system* — Arts. 32, 32a and, through Arts. 40/40a, seal validation — it is **Direct**, and the honest status is **Unknown** because the outcomes are not independently evidenced. The conditional Art. 15 is **Unknown** pending the deployment-scope answer.
