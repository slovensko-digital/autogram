# Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1566 — reference standards for verification of identity and attributes at issuance

## Executive summary

This act fixes the **reference standard for verifying the identity and attributes of the person to whom a qualified certificate or a qualified electronic attestation of attributes (QEAA) is issued**: **ETSI TS 119 461 V2.1.1 (2025-02)**, Annex C clause C.3, with six adaptations. It implements **Article 24(1c)** of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, in the framework amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183. Its operative duties fall on **qualified trust service providers** and, for the automated-use case, **identity proofing service providers (IPSPs)**; there is no obligation on an independent signing/validation application.

Relevance to Autogram is **None / Indirect (context)**, not Direct. Autogram does not verify who a person is, does not collect identity documents or attributes, and issues neither qualified certificates nor QEAAs. What it does is consume the resulting certificate: it reads the local token certificate's subject and issuer, embeds the signing certificate and chain (and, for XAdES, the subject name) into signatures, and exposes subject/issuer locally through the GUI, CLI and `/certificates` API. Inspected evidence establishes those certificate-input behaviours; it establishes no issuance-side identity proofing. The one **Conditional / Unknown** row is recital (5)'s data-protection proviso, which depends on actor-specific processing facts that are not yet documented.

Dates: adopted **29 July 2025**, published **30 July 2025**, entry into force **19 August 2025**, applies from **19 August 2027** (24 months after entry into force, to allow QTSP audit). The full external standards were **not inspected**; if the publisher ever operates an issuance, identity-verification or IPSP role, this assessment must be revisited. Assessment/source access: **8 October 2026**; repository baseline **5ca91d5c**.

### Annex — reference standard for identity and attribute verification

The Annex has no numbered top-level point; it opens with the standard and then lists six numbered adaptations. Adaptation (2) states a **conditional trigger** and two alternative means (a peer-reviewed/certified process, or compliance with clauses C3.1–C3.6); both alternatives are identified individually. The normative clauses of ETSI TS 119 461 are **not reproduced** by the act.

## Coverage and limitations

- **Text obtained.** Complete official English XHTML of Regulation 2025/1566 from the Publications Office/Cellar (`32025R1566?language=eng`, `Accept: application/xhtml+xml`), access date 8 October 2026. It contains the full title, preamble, **all 7 recitals**, **Article 1** (1 unnumbered paragraph), **Article 2** (3 unnumbered paragraphs), the closing adoption/signature formula, the **5 legislative footnotes**, and the complete **single Annex** with its title, opening paragraph and all **6 numbered adaptations**.
- **Enumerated independently from that text:** 3 preamble units; 7 recitals; 2 articles; 1 closing formula; 1 annex with opening paragraph and adaptations (1)–(6), where adaptation (2)'s **two alternative sub-options** are split into separate rows. This yields **23 assessment rows**: 10 preamble/recital rows, 5 article/concluding rows and **8 annex rows**. No source provision was deliberately omitted.
- **Footnotes.** The five preamble footnotes are contextual legislative citations (eIDAS OJ L 257, 28.8.2014; Regulation 2024/1183; GDPR; Directive 2002/58/EC; Regulation 2018/1725) and are represented with the relevant preamble/recital rather than as five new obligations. The annex's bracketed references ([1], [i.1], [i.28]) point to works outside the act and are recorded in the rows that invoke them.
- **Anchor scheme.** Verified against the retrieved Cellar XHTML: ids `art_1`, `art_2`, `rct_1`–`rct_7`, `anx_1`. Links use `#art_1`, `#art_2`, `#rct_1`–`#rct_7` and `#anx_1` for the single annex per the register's EUR-Lex OJ convention. The EUR-Lex OJ/ELI page could **not** be fetched directly (WAF returned HTTP 202), so the OJ-rendered annex anchor was not independently confirmed; a link still opens the act even if the fragment does not scroll.
- **External standards not inspected.** **ETSI TS 119 461 V2.1.1 (2025-02)** and **ETSI EN 319 401 V3.1.1 (2024-06)** are separate works. Only their cited titles/editions and the act's adaptations were read; their clauses (including C3.1–C3.6, VAL-*, USE-*, OVR-* and QTS-*) were **not** inspected and are not silently imported as text of this act. The ENISA report and the Article 24(5) implementing act referred to in the Annex are likewise separate.
- **Not a compliance finding.** No Autogram code implements this act and none is expected to: it creates no application or publisher duty under the confirmed scope. All annex rows are therefore **None / Ignored**, with the annex opening and the recitals/Article 1 treated as **Indirect** context, and only recital (5) flagged **Conditional / Unknown** because it points to data-protection rules that depend on facts not yet documented. **Ignored** here means "outside the confirmed scope of a local signing/validation application", never a deliberate disregard.
- **Outstanding.** A completed subsequent amendment/consolidation-history check; a documented decision on whether the publisher ever operates an issuance, identity-verification or IPSP role (which would move the annex rows from context to potentially applicable); actor/data-processing records for recital (5); and, if that role changes, a dedicated ETSI TS 119 461 / EN 319 401 conformity assessment. No builds, test execution, implementation changes, commits, PDF verification or organisational compliance assertions accompany this reading register.
