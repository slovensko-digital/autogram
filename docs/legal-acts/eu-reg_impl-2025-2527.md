# Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2527 — reference standards for qualified certificates for website authentication

## Executive summary

This act establishes the list of reference standards foreseen by **Article 45(2) of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 (eIDAS)**, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, for **qualified certificates for website authentication (QWACs)**. It names **ETSI EN 319 411-2 V2.6.1 (2025-06)** (with the QNCP-w-gen, QEVCP-w or QNCP-w certificate policy) or **ETSI TS 119 495 V1.7.1 (2024-07)** for QWACs issued for TLS authentication **outside a web-browser**, and **ETSI TS 119 411-5 V2.1.1 (2025-02)** for other QWACs, **including within a web-browser**. The addressees are the **issuers of qualified website-authentication certificates** (qualified trust service providers) and, secondarily, web-browser providers; **issuing website-authentication certificates is not an Autogram duty**.

Autogram is a local desktop signing and verification application (plus a loopback HTTP API and CLI). The inspected source shows it **consumes externally issued signing certificates**, **validates document signatures** and **only reads/lists the user's own certificates after consent**; no certificate-authority, enrollment, issuance or website/TLS-certificate validation path was found. This act therefore has **no Direct obligation** on the application. The act's establishing/context provisions are **Indirect** interpretative input, and the issuance standards themselves address **other actors** and are recorded as **None**. The single **Conditional** item is the GDPR/ePrivacy recital, which depends on data processing in an unresolved QWAC role. The one substantive unknown is whether Autogram is ever used inside a QTSP's QWAC issuance or validation workflow; missing evidence is **not** proof of a violation.

Assessment/source access: **8 October 2026**; repository baseline **5ca91d5c**. Adopted **16 December 2025**, published **17 December 2025**, in force **6 January 2026**, applies from **6 January 2027**.

## Evidence key

All evidence below was inspected on 8 October 2026. Source inspection is not a released-binary audit or proof that tests pass; no builds or tests were run.

- **S — signing uses externally issued certificates; no issuance.** [DssSigningParametersFactory.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/core/DssSigningParametersFactory.java), `createSignatureParameters`, lines 14–27, sets `signatureParameters.setSigningCertificate(key.getCertificate())` and `setCertificateChain(key.getCertificateChain())` — the certificate is supplied by the user's token/keystore, never minted by Autogram. [SigningJob.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/core/SigningJob.java), `signWithKeyAndRespond`, lines 86–109, and `createSignatureService`, lines 131–150, only request a signature over supplied documents. [CertificatesEndpoint.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/server/CertificatesEndpoint.java), lines 17–28, and [CertificatesResponse.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/server/dto/CertificatesResponse.java), lines 6–12, reveal that the HTTP API merely **reads/lists existing certificates** after explicit consent. No CA, enrollment, issuance or TLS server-certificate generation code was found.
- **V — signature validation, not website/TLS-certificate validation.** [SignatureValidator.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/core/SignatureValidator.java), `validate`, lines 76–81, delegates to DSS `validateDocument()`; `initialize`, lines 91–133, configures LOTL/selected countries and online CRL/OCSP; `getTrustedListStatus`, lines 273–302, checks trusted-list completeness. [DSSUtils.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/util/DSSUtils.java), `createDocumentValidator`, lines 40–57, dispatches only to PDF/XML/ASiC/CAdES **signature** validators. No path validates a remote website's TLS certificate.
- **T — TLS/trust material (context, not issuance).** [AutogramServer.java](../../src/main/java/digital/slovensko/autogram/server/AutogramServer.java), `buildServer`, lines 74–112, optionally serves the **loopback API over TLSv1.2** from a user-supplied PKCS12 keystore with `setNeedClientAuth(false)` — a local listener, not a public website. [pom.xml](../../pom.xml), lines 446–473, imports the HARICA RSA root into the bundled JDK `cacerts` for outbound TLS trust; lines 43–49 and 90–97 select DSS **6.5**. Neither is QWAC issuance or browser-side website validation.
- **D — declared application scope.** [README.md](../../README.md), lines 1–6, describes a desktop application for **signing and verifying documents** in accordance with eIDAS, with a separate browser extension; it is not a certificate issuer or a web-browser.

## Provision-by-provision assessment

`None` below means the provision addresses **other actors** (QWAC issuers, browser providers, or the Commission), consistently with the confirmed scope; those rows are **Ignored**, not "knowingly disregarded". `Indirect` marks establishing/commencement/interpretative context that is not itself a duty on this publisher. The **issuance-standards rows are None** because, as the inspected code shows, Autogram does not issue, enroll for, or validate website-authentication certificates.

## Coverage and limitations

- Enumerated directly from the official English act (CELEX 32025R2527) obtained via the Cellar XHTML resource, not copied from the existing compliance report: **3 preamble units; 7 recitals; 2 articles** (Article 1: one unnumbered paragraph; Article 2: three unnumbered paragraphs); **1 binding formula (Article 2(3)); 1 adoption/signature formula; 1 Annex** with a title and **2 numbered points with 3 indents**. These produce **21 assessment rows**: **10 preamble/recital rows**, **5 article/concluding rows** and **6 annex rows**.
- Every recital (1)–(7), every article paragraph and every annex point/indent is individually identifiable; the act's paragraphs are unnumbered, so they are labelled `unnumbered paragraph 1`–`3`. Cross-links in the prose do not replace assessment rows.
- Reference coverage: the Annex names **three external specifications** — EN 319 411-2 V2.6.1 (2025-06), TS 119 495 V1.7.1 (2024-07) and TS 119 411-5 V2.1.1 (2025-02) — each represented by its own row. Preamble footnotes **(1)–(7)** are legislative citations (eIDAS 910/2014, PSD2 2015/2366, 2024/1183, GDPR 2016/679, ePrivacy 2002/58, 2018/1725, EDPS comments) represented with the preamble/recital context, not as seven additional obligations.
- **Anchor scheme** (`#art_1`, `#art_2`, `#rct_1`–`#rct_7`, single-annex `#anx_1`) was verified against the IDs in the retrieved Cellar XHTML; the act has one unnamed annex, so `#anx_1` — not a roman numeral — is the correct publisher anchor.
- **The three referenced ETSI standards were not inspected.** They are separate works and are not reproduced or silently imported; any issuer/QWAC conformity conclusion needs the full standards and the issuers' certification evidence.
- Access limitation: the direct `eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/.../oj` HTML request returned an empty body (WAF); the assessment relies on the structurally identical **official Cellar XHTML**, whose complete body text (including footnotes) was retrieved. No downloadable PDF was independently verified.

Absent that, no application duty under this act is established, and missing evidence is not proof of a violation.
