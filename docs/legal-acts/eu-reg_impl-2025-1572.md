# Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/1572 — notification and verification of qualified trust services

## Executive summary

This act lays down the format and procedures for a trust service provider to **notify** a supervisory body of its intention to start providing a **qualified trust service**, and for the supervisory body to **verify** the provider's compliance before granting qualified status (Article 21(4) of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, in coordination with the NIS 2 Directive (EU) 2022/2555). Its addressees are explicitly **Member State supervisory bodies** (Articles 1, 2 and 4) and **trust service providers** that choose to notify (Article 3). It does not address software publishers, signing applications or their users.

Under the confirmed scope the organisation **is not a qualified trust-service provider**, does not issue qualified certificates and operates **no** Autogram service receiving users' documents or personal data. It therefore has no notification, verification-methodology, transparency or conformity-assessment duty of its own under this act; those provisions are **None / Ignored** for the publisher, and the act is relevant to Autogram only **indirectly** as context: the qualified certificates, national trusted-list entries and trust-service identifiers that Autogram consumes on the evidenced path are the *outputs* of the notification and qualification process described here. Inspected source shows local signing with a user-selected token, EU LOTL/national trusted-list loading for validation and DSS-based qualification presentation — none of which is notifying or operating a qualified trust service.

Recital (4) (GDPR/ePrivacy) is the one **Conditional / Unknown** row, because local certificate/revocation processing exists while the operator's data-protection records were not reviewed. Overall fulfilment for this publisher is **None / Ignored** on every operative provider/supervisor duty; that reflects confirmed scope, not a judgement that the act is unimportant to interoperability. Assessment/source access: **8 October 2026**; repository baseline **5ca91d5c**. Adopted **29 July 2025**, published **30 July 2025**, in force **19 August 2025**, applicable from **19 August 2026**.

## Coverage and limitations

- Enumerated independently from the official act, not copied from the existing compliance report: **6 recitals; 5 articles; 4 preamble units; 1 adoption formula; 2 concluding-formula rows**. These produce **42 assessment rows**: **10 preamble/recital rows**, 2 Article 1 rows, 6 Article 2 rows, 13 Article 3 rows (1 opening + 8 numbered points, with point (7) split into 4 unnumbered dash subpoints), 6 Article 4 rows (2 paragraphs + 4 lettered points in paragraph 2), 3 Article 5 rows and 2 concluding rows.
- Article 3's opening line and structural headings — Article 3(7), Article 4(2) and the Article 2 opening — are parent labels, not duplicate duties; each individually identifiable child (Article 3(1)–(8) with (7)'s four dash subpoints; Article 4(1)–(2)(a)–(d); Article 2(1)–(5)) carries its own row.
- Reference coverage: the act's **five footnotes** are contextual citations to eIDAS (1), GDPR (2), the ePrivacy Directive (3), Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 (4) and the NIS 2 Directive (5). They are represented with the relevant recital/preamble rows and are **not** counted as new obligations; no implementing annex or technical standard is incorporated by this act.
- Obtained text: the complete official English XHTML, including every recital, article paragraph/letter and all footnotes, was retrieved from the Cellar CELEX endpoint and read in full. The EUR-Lex ELI/OJ **HTML page was WAF-blocked** in-session (a `challenge-container` response), so live anchor resolution and the rendered-page cross-check could not be performed; the `#art_<n>`/`#rct_<n>` scheme follows the publisher convention and the sibling [2025/1945](eu-reg_impl-2025-1945.md) document. This access limitation is stated rather than claiming independent PDF verification.
- **No external referenced act was inspected in full.** Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 Article 21(4), Article 20(4), Article 22(5) and Article 24(2)(fa)/(i); Directive (EU) 2022/2555 Articles 8(1) and 21; the GDPR/ePrivacy data-protection acts; and Regulation (EU) 2018/1725 remain separate works requiring their own documents. Their wording is not silently imported as text of this act.
- Technical evidence is confined to the consuming client: local signing through a user-selected token (S), EU LOTL/national trusted-list loading for validation (V) and DSS-based qualification presentation (U). None of these constitutes notifying or operating a qualified trust service; no repository code implements Article 1–4 procedures, and searching `src/` showed no notification, supervisory or conformity-assessment-report functionality. Eight countries remain selected by default (V), which affects validation coverage, not this act's applicability.
- Outstanding: confirmation of the organisation's role if any paid trust service is ever operated; actor-specific data-protection records for Recital (4); and a subsequent amendment-history verification of the act. Inspected source proves narrow client behaviour only. No builds, test execution, implementation changes, commits or organisational compliance assertions accompany this reading register.
